# Weekly Executive Update

**When to use it:** Send a short executive update every week to the customer's sponsor and your own leadership, for the whole engagement. Executives do not have time for details. They want to know: are we on track, what changed, what do you need from me, and is there anything I should worry about. A good weekly update builds trust, so that when bad news comes, the sponsor already believes you.

**Who reads it:** The customer's executive sponsor (often a VP or C-level), your account executive, your engagement manager, and your own leadership. It is often forwarded, so it must make sense on its own.

**How long it should be:** Readable in 2 minutes. Half a page to one page. If it scrolls a lot, it is a status report, not an executive update.

---

## The template

### Subject line
*Good looks like: status color and the one headline in the subject, so the reader gets it even without opening the email.*

[Project name] weekly update, week [N]: [Green / Amber / Red], [headline]

### 1. Overall status
*Good looks like: one color and one sentence saying why. Be honest; Amber early is better than Red late.*

Status: [Green / Amber / Red]. [One-sentence reason.]

### 2. Headline this week
*Good looks like: the single most important thing, stated as a business result, not a technical task.*

[Headline]

### 3. Progress against goals
*Good looks like: 3 to 4 key numbers compared to targets, the same numbers every week.*

| Measure | Target | This week | Last week |
|---|---|---|---|

### 4. Decisions or help needed
*Good looks like: a specific ask with a deadline and the cost of not deciding. If there is no ask, say "None this week."*

- [Ask], needed by [date], because [impact].

### 5. Top risks
*Good looks like: at most 3 risks, each with what you are doing about it.*

- [Risk]: [mitigation]

### 6. Next week
*Good looks like: 2 or 3 things that will be done, so next week's update can confirm them.*

- [Item]

## Worked example: Northwind Logistics

**Subject:** Northwind invoice assistant weekly update, week 6: Green, accuracy target met and pilot started

### 1. Overall status
Status: Green. We met the accuracy target and the pilot started on schedule.

### 2. Headline this week
The assistant now reads invoices correctly 97.4% of the time on the 12 fields that matter most, above the 97% target, and the evaluation report (D2) was accepted on June 10. Shadow-mode pilot with 3 carriers began on June 8.

### 3. Progress against goals

| Measure | Target | This week | Last week |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical field accuracy | 97% | 97.4% | 96.1% |
| Invoices needing no edits (pilot) | 60% by week 10 | 52% | not started |
| Handling time per invoice | under 3 min | 3.4 min (pilot clerks) | 7 min baseline |
| Schedule | Go-live week 8 | On track | On track |

### 4. Decisions or help needed
- Dana: please approve moving 2 more clerks into the pilot next week, needed by June 16. Without them we will not have enough reviewer feedback to tune the system before go-live in week 8.

- Clerk confidence: some clerks still worry about their roles. Luis is holding a team session on June 17 about moving freed time to carrier disputes.

### 6. Next week

- Finish the daily batch approval screen for drafts.
- Go/no-go review for week 8 go-live on June 19.

## Common mistakes

- Sending a list of technical tasks ("refactored the validator") instead of business progress.
- Staying Green until the week everything goes Red. Sponsors lose trust fast when surprised.
- Changing which numbers you report each week, so trends are hidden.
- Asking for help vaguely ("we need more support") instead of a specific decision with a date.
- Writing too much. If the sponsor needs details, link to the status report.
