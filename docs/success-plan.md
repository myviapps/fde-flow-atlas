# Customer Success Plan and Value Tracker

**When to use it:** Start the success plan in the last two weeks before go-live, while the customer still remembers why they bought. Update the value tracker every month after go-live, and re-read the whole plan before every executive business review (EBR) and every renewal conversation. The plan answers three questions in writing: what did the customer say success would look like, what has actually been delivered so far, and what happens next. It stops "we think it is going well" from being an opinion and turns it into numbers the customer's sponsor can repeat to their own boss.

**Who reads it:** The customer's executive sponsor and project owner; the customer success manager (CSM) or account executive on your side, who owns the renewal; the FDE while they are still on the account; and, after handoff, the customer's own team lead. A finance contact at the customer often reads the value tracker to check the savings maths.

**How long it should be:** The plan is 2 pages. The value tracker is one table that grows by one row a month. If the plan passes 3 pages, nobody will update it, and an out-of-date plan is worse than none.

---

## The template

### 1. Header
*Good looks like: the customer, the contract dates and one named owner on each side, so it is clear who keeps the plan alive after the FDE leaves.*

- Customer: [company name]
- Solution(s) live: [names and go-live dates]
- Contract term and renewal date: [start, end, notice period for non-renewal]
- Customer executive sponsor: [name, role]
- Customer day-to-day owner: [name, role]
- Our owner after go-live (CSM): [name]
- FDE (until handoff date): [name, handoff date]
- Plan version and date: [v1, date]

### 2. Success criteria the customer signed up for
*Good looks like: 2 to 4 measurable outcomes in the customer's own words, each with a baseline (the number before), a target and a date. Copy them from the business case or statement of work. Never invent new ones here.*

| # | Outcome (customer's words) | Metric | Baseline | Target | Target date | Who measures |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [outcome] | [metric and unit] | [number, source, date] | [number] | [date] | [name] |

### 3. Value tracker (update monthly)
*Good looks like: every number has a source someone at the customer can open (a report, a query, a time study). Show the calculation, not just the result. Mark estimates as estimates.*

| Month | Volume | Metric this month | Baseline | Change per unit | Value in money or hours | Source | Confidence (measured, sampled, estimated) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [month] | [units] | [number] | [number] | [number] | [number] | [report name] | [level] |

- Running total of value realised so far: [amount]
- Cost of the solution so far (licence plus services): [amount]
- Value realised as a share of the business case forecast: [percent]

### 4. Adoption and health
*Good looks like: a few signals that show whether people really use the solution, checked against the risk signals you agreed on.*

| Signal | Green | Amber | Red | Current | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly active users or volume | [threshold] | [threshold] | [threshold] | [number] | [up, flat, down] |
| Share of work handled without a human fix | | | | | |
| Open support tickets older than 14 days | | | | | |
| Sponsor engagement (last meeting date) | | | | | |
| Champions still in role | | | | | |

### 5. Risks to renewal
*Good looks like: honest. Name the risk, the evidence, an owner and a date. Do not hide amber items until they are red.*

| Risk | Evidence | Owner | Action and date |
|---|---|---|---|
| [risk] | [what you saw] | [name] | [action, date] |

### 6. Next use cases and expansion candidates
*Good looks like: 2 or 3 candidates found from real customer requests or from the data, each with a named customer champion and a rough size. Not a wish list.*

| Candidate | Who asked for it | Rough value | Ready? (data, owner, budget) | Next step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [use case] | [name] | [estimate] | [yes, partly, no] | [step, date] |

### 7. Meeting rhythm and next EBR
*Good looks like: dates in the diary, not intentions.*

- Monthly check-in: [day, attendees]
- Quarterly executive business review: [next date, sponsor confirmed?]
- Renewal conversation starts: [date, at least 120 days before the renewal date]

### 8. Reference and case study permission
*Good looks like: agreed only after value is proven, in writing, with the customer's legal or communications team.*

- Willing to act as a reference: [no, ask later, yes for calls, yes for a public case study]
- Approver at the customer: [name]
- Facts they have approved for public use: [list]

## Worked example: Brannock Logistics

Context: Atlas AI's invoice-extraction assistant went live at Brannock Logistics on July 10, 2026. Priya Shah, Forward Deployed Engineer at Atlas AI, wrote this plan with Dana Whitlock, the customer success manager who takes over the account after the FDE's handoff. The plan below is version 2, dated October 2, 2026, after the September numbers came in.

### 1. Header
- Customer: Brannock Logistics
- Solution live: Invoice Extraction Assistant, live July 10, 2026; Carrier Payment Status agent in shadow mode since September 21
- Contract term and renewal date: February 1, 2026 to January 31, 2027; 60 days' notice to not renew (by December 2, 2026)
- Customer executive sponsor: Beth Reyes, VP Finance
- Customer day-to-day owner: Luis Ortega, AP Manager
- Our owner after go-live: Dana Whitlock, CSM
- FDE until handoff: Priya Shah, handoff on October 30, 2026
- Plan version and date: v2, October 2, 2026

### 2. Success criteria
| # | Outcome (Brannock's words) | Metric | Baseline | Target | Target date | Who measures |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Stop typing invoices by hand" | Clerk minutes per invoice | 11.0 (time study, Feb 2026, 40 invoices) | 3.0 | Sept 30, 2026 | Luis Ortega |
| 2 | "Fewer wrong payments" | Invoices needing a payment correction | 2.4% | 1.0% | Sept 30, 2026 | AP audit report |
| 3 | "Pay carriers on time" | Invoices paid within terms | 84% | 95% | Dec 31, 2026 | ERP aging report |

### 3. Value tracker
Assumption agreed with finance: a clerk hour costs Brannock $38 (salary plus benefits). Forecast in the business case: 9,500 invoices a month, 8.0 minutes saved each, which is 1,267 hours or about $48,100 a month.

| Month | Volume | Minutes per invoice | Baseline | Change per unit | Value | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aug 2026 | 9,100 | 5.6 | 11.0 | 5.4 min | 819 hours, $31,100 | Time log export | Measured |
| Sep 2026 | 9,300 | 4.5 | 11.0 | 6.5 min | 1,008 hours, $38,300 | Time log export | Measured |

- Running total of value realised so far (Aug and Sep): $69,400
- Cost of the solution so far (licence plus services, Feb to Sep): $121,000
- Value realised against the forecast for Sep: 80% ($38,300 of $48,100)

Reading the numbers: the plan does not claim success yet. Minutes per invoice (4.5) has not reached the 3.0 target, and the running value is below the cost so far. The trend is right (5.6 to 4.5 in one month), so the plan sets the action for section 5.

### 4. Adoption and health
| Signal | Green | Amber | Red | Current | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Invoices processed without a clerk fix | 85% or more | 70 to 84% | under 70% | 78% | Up (71% in Aug) |
| Open support tickets older than 14 days | 0 to 2 | 3 to 5 | 6 or more | 4 | Flat |
| Sponsor meeting in the last 45 days | Yes | 46 to 75 days | over 75 days | 38 days ago | Ok |
| Champions still in role | All | One left | Two or more left | All (Luis Ortega, Ana Silva) | Ok |

### 5. Risks to renewal
| Risk | Evidence | Owner | Action and date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Value below cost so far; Beth Reyes may ask why | Running value $69,400 against $121,000 cost | Dana Whitlock | Bring the monthly trend and the fix plan to the EBR on Oct 22 |
| Fix rate of 22% held back by scanned invoices from 3 carriers | Error log: 61% of fixes come from 3 carriers | Priya Shah | Add a scan-cleaning step and retest by Oct 23 |
| Ana Silva is the only Brannock engineer who knows the pipeline | Handoff review, Sep 25 | Luis Ortega | Name a backup by Oct 15 |

### 6. Next use cases
| Candidate | Who asked for it | Rough value | Ready? | Next step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Carrier payment status email agent | Luis Ortega (about 4,100 emails a month) | About 220 clerk hours a month, $8,400 | Partly: spec done, in shadow mode | Move to 10% rollout on Oct 12 |
| Purchase order matching | Beth Reyes | Not sized | No: no data owner yet | Discovery session in November |

### 7. Meeting rhythm
- Monthly check-in: first Thursday, Luis Ortega, Dana Whitlock, Priya Shah until handoff
- Executive business review: October 22, 2026, Beth Reyes confirmed
- Renewal conversation starts: October 5, 2026, which is 118 days before the renewal date, about two days short of the 120-day target, so Dana Whitlock has booked it for the same week and will move it up if the sponsor is free

### 8. Reference permission
- Willing to act as a reference: not yet; Beth Reyes said to ask again after the 3.0 minute target is met
- Approver at the customer: Beth Reyes (with Brannock communications)
- Facts approved for public use: none yet

## Common mistakes

- **Claiming value without a baseline.** "We saved a lot of time" cannot be checked. Measure the before number in week 1, or the tracker is fiction.
- **Counting hours saved as money that will show up in the budget.** Saved hours only become cash if headcount or overtime changes. Say "capacity freed" unless finance agrees otherwise.
- **Changing the success criteria to match the results.** If a target was wrong, say so and get the sponsor to agree to a new one in writing.
- **Hiding amber items until they are red.** A risk the customer sees in the plan first is a shared problem; one they find themselves is a trust problem.
- **Starting the renewal conversation at the renewal date.** Begin at least 120 days before it, when there is still time to fix what the customer is unhappy about.
- **Asking for a reference before value is proven.** It makes the customer feel used and often gets a polite no.
